# Pump.fun's documented edges belong to insiders, not latecomers

No published study finds an after-fee edge on the pump.fun curve, out of sample, for an outside trader running a 1-2 s bot on free data. Every positive result needs one of three things: insider position (devs and their bundles made at least $579M), raw speed (25,882 sniper wallets made about 3.5% on capital with unsold tokens marked at zero, before tips), or hindsight (wallets, teams or thresholds chosen on the same data they are scored on). Our own results match that picture: a blind 30 s entry loses about 3% per trade, the mined rules failed out of sample, and copying a fast bot one slot late flipped +6.6% to -3.9%. The literature explains why. The counterparty to a late entry is usually a dev, bundle or sniper about to sell. Devs sell within a minute on 65% of launches. Curve price impact plus the round-trip fee of about 2.5% is roughly as large as a fast leader's whole gross edge. Three openings remain plausible for a bot like ours, and none is proven. The first is copying slow profitable wallets, whose edge should survive a 1-2 s delay; the literature has not tested this. The second is the post-graduation BOOST window, the only strategy with measured on-chain profit, though at about 70 SOL per trade and with an unknown status in November 2026. The third is using deployer, bundle, Mayhem and dev-sell labels as avoid filters, which cuts losses but has never been shown to turn them into profit. Sniping, market making on the curve, and LP on fresh PumpSwap pools should be dropped.

## Every measured profit pool sits upstream of a 2-second bot

### Academic and analytics studies agree that naive entries lose and that models do not generalize

The strongest academic paper on graduation covers 655,770 tokens launched in September 2025. It finds that **only 0.63% graduated** (2.55% of tokens with at least 30 swaps), with a median of about 457 trades and 4.4 minutes to graduation. Its key contribution is a breakeven rule for buy-and-hold to graduation: **an entry at virtual SOL level vSol pays only if P(graduate) ≥ (vSol/115)²**. The baseline graduation curve sits below that line at every level. Only small subsets get close: tokens with a non-bot trade share of about 0.3 near graduation, and the top-10 creators, where the paper itself is internally inconsistent ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)). The best single predictor is "liquidity velocity", meaning a token reaches a given SOL level in fewer trades. No out-of-sample accuracy is reported for it. A pre-registered audit of 749,816 mints from May-June 2026 shows why that omission matters. Its graduation model scored **AUROC 0.859 in development and 0.464 on the following 14 days**, a confidence interval that contains a coin flip, and its calibration collapsed ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02823)). This is the same failure our roughly 1,000 mined rules and 54 dip setups hit. It looks like a structural property of the market, not a flaw in our method: patterns go stale within days.

The best-designed outsider test in the notes is Arrow Algo's pre-registered paper-trading lab (17-26 Oct 2026). It bought every gated token at 30 minutes of age, with 20 s latency, 1.25% fees per leg and 1% slippage. Mean returns were **-31.4% at 1 h, -61.8% at 6 h and -55.7% at 24 h** across 1,411 tokens. None of its 416 strategies had a significantly positive mean out of sample, and its best in-sample strategy (+85.2%) lost 81.2% out of sample ([Arrow Algo](https://arrowalgo.com/solana-memecoin-trading/)). Earlier entries do not escape this. The manipulation census "Meme Coin Factories" (about 15M coins, accepted at ACM CCS'26) finds that strategic actors bypass the UI and trade "in a highly automated and low-latency fashion", and that manipulation-as-a-service tooling is sold openly ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10246)). One caution on sources: a July 2026 preprint on "coordinated sniper cohorts" was withdrawn after 43% of its buy rows turned out to be sells, so its figures should not be used ([arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02795)).

Headline wallet-profitability statistics mislead. CoinGecko's monthly share of profitable wallets rose from **30.08% (June 2025) to 73.28% (April 2026)**. Over the same period monthly active wallets fell from a 5.2M peak to 1.8M, and the researcher could not explain the rise ([Yahoo Finance/BeInCrypto](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/73-pump-fun-traders-profit-094850577.html)). The likelier reading is that losing traders left and the remaining market got more professional. The measure also counts realized PnL only, so unsold losers drop out. Of 3.14M active wallets in April, 65.1% made $1-500 and only 5.37% made more than $1k. In January 2025, only about 0.41% of 13.5M wallets had ever realized more than $10k ([TradingView/Cointelegraph](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:0f6859ba7094b:0-99-6-of-pump-fun-traders-haven-t-locked-in-over-10k-in-profits-data/)).

### Sniping pays a few percent, to a few hundred wallets, after an infrastructure arms race

Bitquery's census of **8.2M launches (Oct 2025 to Jun 2026)** is the only method-transparent study of sniper profit. It ranks the profit pools clearly. Devs plus their bundled wallets made **at least $579M** after fees and came out ahead on 71% of bundled launches. Devs without bundles made $84.3M. The 25,882 sniper wallets, defined as wallets buying in the first three blocks of launches by at least 10 different devs, spent $440M. They made **$31.5M on sold tokens, or $15.2M with unsold holdings at zero**, which is about 3.5% on capital. That excludes Jito tips and failed transactions, so the true net figure is lower. Only 54% of sniper wallets came out ahead, and the top 100 took $13.1M. Unlinked early buyers made $22.7M in total ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)). Faster Solana blocks did not thin out the race. Block-0 buying by non-devs fell from 44% to about 31-35%, but the share of buying inside about 1.3 s stayed near 52-53% ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-study-finds-rapid-pump-fun-buying-persisted-as-blocks-sped-up/)). Infrastructure vendors say a 0.001 SOL tip "is unlikely to be competitive" ([Helius Sender docs](https://www.helius.dev/docs/pt-BR/sending-transactions/sender-max)), and a reverted bundle still costs its tip ([RPCFast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-improve-your-jito-bundle-landing-rate-on-solana)).

This explains our -3% at 30 s. A 1-2 s bot arrives 3-5 blocks into a coin, just as the earliest buyers start distributing, and **devs make their first sale within 1 minute on 65% of launches** ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)). Sniping is not a retail strategy at our latency, and no published source shows profitability by individual slot. Our data can fill that gap. Compute realized PnL per wallet class for entry buckets at slot 0, slots 1-2, slots 3-10, 10 s, 30 s and 60 s or later, with unsold tokens marked at zero. Then decompose who sells into our 30 s entries: what share of the sell volume after our entry comes from dev wallets and from buyers in blocks 0-2. This is a diagnostic, not a strategy. It tells us whether any time bucket that a non-insider can reach is positive.

## Copying only works for wallets slow enough to outlast our delay

### The copy-trading literature measures decay that matches our one-slot flip

A copier on a bonding curve pays the leader's own price impact, plus whatever other buyers add during the delay, plus about 2-2.5% in fees. The best measured result comes from a 6,000-coin study with a chronological 70/15/15 split. Smart-money wallets chosen by an LLM framework averaged **+14% per trade, but the estimated copier return was only +3%** after the 1% fee and an "imitation penalty". The statistical baselines, which ranked wallets on past returns with a t-stat gate, **found profitable wallets whose copiers lost money** ([arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)). A simulation of 100,000 copy attempts on the top 50 leaders puts the copier's win rate at **82% at zero latency, 28% at 1 s and 4% at 5 s**. Its latency values are scenario inputs, not measurements ([Kurnovskii](https://romankurnovskii.com/en/blog/pumpfun-copy-trading-feasibility/)). A two-week study of 384M swaps found that entering 2 minutes after large buyer clusters lost **-41% per token**. Following clusters with good track records still lost 11%, and only tightly connected groups made about +10% per token, on just 16 tokens ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/35409)).

The one encouraging result comes from a different market: on a public-identity DEX order book, wallet skill rankings persisted across adjacent 10-day windows with a **rank correlation of 0.52**, mostly in the top decile ([arXiv 2608.04373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04373)). Skill can persist. The question is whether it persists in wallets whose trades we can still copy profitably.

### Slow non-deployer wallets are the untested case, and the one most worth testing

The decay math points to a clear target. A leader who enters minutes after launch, holds 5-10 minutes or longer, and trades small relative to curve depth leaves most of its edge intact after a 1-2 s delay. Kurnovskii advises against copying wallets that hold less than 5 minutes for the same reason ([Kurnovskii](https://romankurnovskii.com/en/blog/pumpfun-copy-trading-feasibility/)). ARu4n5mF in our own data fits this profile: it enters about 3.5 minutes after launch, holds about 12 minutes, and made +85 SOL over 131 coins. No published study covers this case. What we need to learn is whether such wallets exist in numbers and stay profitable from one period to the next. A wallet's profitability is necessary but not sufficient. The selection rule must score whether the edge survives **our** entry price, rebuilt from the reserves at our fill slot.

The test is a walk-forward study. In period A, label every wallet: deployer or bundle (launch transaction plus the next four transactions in the launch slot), sniper (first 3 blocks across 10 or more devs), wash or bump (same amount bought and sold within 2 slots or fewer), or "other". Keep "other" wallets with at least 30 coins that pass a t-stat gate, and record their entry ages and hold times. In period B, with no reselection, replay copy entries at delays of 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 slots and at 2 s, 5 s and 30 s. Reprice every fill from the reconstructed reserves, including the trades in between and our own impact. Compare three exits: mirroring the leader's sell, holding for the leader's median hold time, and our dev-sell rule. Report results with and without the top 10 trades, against random cohorts with the same activity level, as Gafarov does ([arXiv 2611.11436](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11436)). Then plot the decay curve separately for slow and fast wallets. Add two bait checks: how much buying lands 1-3 slots after the leader's buys, and whether the leader sells into it. **Fourteen hours is not enough.** Period A needs about 7-14 days and period B another 7, rolled forward weekly. Gaining that duration is the main reason to keep the collector running.

### KOL front-running is already done by the KOLs themselves

Bubblemaps' November 2026 investigation found that Anglio's five linked wallets bought before **61% of his calls, about 20 s ahead**, and sold a few minutes later. 0xEthan typically sold within 3 minutes of a call, with three side wallets buying in the same second as his public wallet. He also earned about $80k from pump.fun callout rewards, an incentive that does not depend on followers making money ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/bubblemaps-investigates-crypto-kol-moves-onchain/)). The slower evidence is just as negative. In a peer-reviewed study of 36,000 influencer tweets, returns were **+1.83% on day 1 and -18.90% after 90 days** ([HBS Working Knowledge](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/when-celebrity-crypto-influencers-rake-in-cash-investors-lose-big)). In a study of more than 1,500 promoted memecoins, 86% fell 90% or more within 3 months ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/most-promoted-memecoins-on-x-plummet-by-90/)). A bot reacting 1-2 s after a KOL's public wallet buys is about 20 s behind the side wallets and minutes from their exit. KOL wallets therefore belong in an avoid list, not a follow list. Free sources exist to build that list: the Solana Tracker KOL leaderboard endpoints ([Solana Tracker docs](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/pnl-v2/leaderboards)) and MadeOnSol's tracker of more than 1,000 KOL wallets ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/kol-tracker)). Wallets that are profitable in our data but missing from these public boards are probably less crowded and less likely to be bait.

## Deployer, bundle and volume-bot labels are filters, not signals

Insider detection works cheaply on data we already have. Bitquery's rule is simple: wallets in the launch transaction or the next four transactions of the launch slot are dev-owned. In a check, **185 of 186** such buys sat in the launch's Jito bundle, and adding SOL-funding links found only **0.4% more** bundled launches ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)). Wash trading, meaning the same address buying and selling the same amount in one transaction, makes up **at least 17% of trading transactions but only about 1.3% of SOL volume**. Wash-traded coins graduate at 2.0% versus 0.90% for others. Copycat coins are at least 10% of launches and graduate at 0.86%, against 9.20% for the originals they copy ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)). The authors stress that a higher graduation rate does not make wash-traded coins good buys. The volume is engineered to attract exactly the buyers it attracts.

Trading alongside insiders has no out-of-sample support. Gafarov's funding-network "team-following" study, with entries 4 s after the team at 0.1 SOL, ended above 1 SOL of equity in all 8 windows that had a team. But unrealized marks were 17-94% of equity, free cash ended above 1 SOL in only 5 windows, and under causal recognition **9 of 19 teams became identifiable only after their last launch**. The author calls the results "not executable profits" ([arXiv 2611.11436](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11436)). Avoiding insiders, by contrast, measurably cuts losses but does not remove them. In Arrow Algo's lab, a first-time-creator, no-bundle filter lost 44% at 6 h versus 82% for the other launches ([Arrow Algo](https://arrowalgo.com/solana-memecoin-trading/)). So these labels belong in the feature set of every other test, as exclusions (dev-owned share above X% at entry, a known factory buy size like our 2.21 SOL across 30-50 coins, wash share) and as exit triggers (dev has sold, which our dev-sell rule v1 already uses). On their own they will not produce a positive strategy. The one gap our data cannot close is gradual bundles and factories funded through several hops, which need SOL-transfer edges. Collect those only for candidate wallets, via RPC `getSignaturesForAddress`.

## Graduation holds the only measured retail-latency edge, and it is a protocol subsidy

### Depth, not price, breaks at migration

At 115 virtual SOL (85 real) the curve completes and migrates into a canonical PumpSwap pool with burned LP tokens. In the words of the arXiv authors, "the token price is kept continuous at graduation by design, but market depth is not" ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)). Our own arithmetic from those reserves: selling 100M tokens at the end of the curve (115 SOL / 280M tokens) returns about 30.3 SOL, while selling them into the fresh pool (85 SOL / 207M) returns about 27.7 SOL. That makes post-migration exits **roughly 8-9% worse**, and the gap grows with size. Large holders therefore have a reason to dump into the last stretch of the curve, and 92.22% of active tokens show at least one dump event, clustered at higher SOL levels ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)). Fees give no relief. A fresh graduate at about 400 SOL market cap sits in PumpSwap's top tier of **1.25%**, the same as the curve, and only **0.02% of that goes to LPs** ([Pump.fun help center](https://intercom.help/pumpfun-web/en/articles/11002413-transaction-fees-on-pump-fun)). Synthetic migration further shrinks the old pool-open race. A `buy_v3` that completes the curve keeps filling at the future pool's prices, so the pool opens exactly where that buy stopped ([pump-public-docs](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/SYNTHETIC_MIGRATION.md)). Mayhem coins are excluded from this mechanism.

### BOOST created a predictable 5-minute bid, and completers farmed it

Since **21 July 2026**, BOOST runs a TWAP buy-and-burn of about **17.6 SOL over the first ~5 minutes** after each standard migration (Mayhem coins are excluded), against a pool of about 67-85 SOL ([OnchainDivers](https://onchaindivers.substack.com/p/the-176-sol-ghost-reverse-engineering); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/409815/pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives)). That is the one known, mechanical order flow in the graduation window. It was exploited at once. Graduation rates jumped from 2.5% to a 6.7% day. About 230 migrations came from the stalled-curve band in one day, against a usual 2-6. Traced "completer" trades, which buy out a stalled curve and sell into the BOOST bid, made **+2.7 SOL on 73 SOL and +4.15 SOL on 68.4 SOL**, about 4-6%, and the author puts the realistic range at +2 to +4 SOL. A small set of sniper wallets hits almost every completion **0-6 s after pool creation**; one wallet appeared in 13 of 14 traced tokens ([OnchainDivers](https://onchaindivers.substack.com/p/the-176-sol-ghost-reverse-engineering)). Graduations completed in three curve trades or fewer rose from 2% to 33% of all graduations ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-launchpad-year)).

This is the only strategy in the notes with measured on-chain profit, and its timing works for a slow bot, because the completer chooses when to complete. But it needs about 70 SOL per trade, which is 140 times our size. It depends on a subsidy pump.fun can remove, and its status in November 2026 is unknown. A smaller variant fits our budget: hold 0.5 SOL of a curve entering its last few SOL, then sell into the BOOST TWAP instead of into the post-BOOST pool. Whether the bid outweighs the dump from end-of-curve holders has never been measured.

### What happens after graduation has not been measured publicly

No source publishes the share of graduates trading above their graduation price at 1 h or 24 h. Survival data are mixed. 43% of graduates still traded in their fifth week, and 98% of pools never had liquidity removed ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-launchpad-year)). But a Medium study of 1,634 graduates, seen only through search snippets, found 46.6% "under $100" at 24-48 h ([Medium/Coinmonks](https://medium.com/coinmonks/what-happens-after-a-pump-fun-coin-graduates-we-tracked-1-634-of-them-61efab687f1b)). The "buy the post-migration dip", "fade graduation" and "buy the last SOL, sell at migration" plays have **no after-fee measurement at all**. Each needs a gross move well above 3-4% to clear 2.5% in fees plus priority fees. Our curve data can test the entry side now. It can show net flow, sell share and price path in real-SOL bins of 70-75, 75-80 and 80-85, how many stalled curves sit at 50-80 SOL, and who completes them. The exit side needs a PumpSwap collector. The pool address is deterministic from the mint, so we can subscribe before graduation ([SolanaTracker docs](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/pumpfun)) and record every swap for at least 60 minutes: slot, wallet, side, SOL, tokens, post-swap reserves, fees, the migration transaction's initial reserves, and the BOOST agent's buys. With only hundreds to about 2,000 graduations a day, this should fit on free RPC.

### Market making and LP are closed doors on fresh pools

The curve has no order book and no spread to capture. Market making there reduces to round trips at about 2.5% each, so curve "MM" activity consists of volume and bump bots ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)). On PumpSwap, the migration liquidity is burned, so an outside LP would have to add to an existing pool. At fresh-graduate market caps that LP earns **0.02%** of volume ([Pump.fun help center](https://intercom.help/pumpfun-web/en/articles/11002413-transaction-fees-on-pump-fun)). Against that, impermanent loss alone is about 42% if the token falls 90% ([zelcore](https://zelcore.io/academy/defi/impermanent-loss-liquidity-provider-risk)). The LP fee only rises to 0.20% above about 420 SOL of market cap. No measured LP PnL study exists; the "$125/day" claims come from bump-bot marketing ([Medium](https://medium.com/@jump_bit/pumpswap-lp-fees-explained-how-to-earn-125-day-from-your-memecoin-pool-6cf6b2c2a918)). Drop LP.

## Mayhem and the fee mechanics give us a filter and a small discount, not a strategy

### The Mayhem agent resets reserves, so its price path is effectively noise to us

The Mayhem flag in our data is almost certainly `is_mayhem_mode` from `create_v2`. Mayhem coins get a second 1B tokens held by the agent, and pump.fun's fee-free agent trades them before burning what is left ([pump-public-docs](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/instructions/COIN_CREATION.md)). The agent's trading account is **BwWK17cbHxwWBKZkUYvzxLcNQ1YVyaFezduWbtm2de6s**, the `sol-vault` PDA of the Mayhem program. That same PDA signs `set_mayhem_virtual_params`, which **rewrites a curve's virtual reserves** ([pump IDL](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/idl/pump.json)). This explains the ±50% jumps in vSOL and the readings below 30 SOL in our data, which x·y=k cannot produce. Bitquery documents the effect. A tiny agent buy moved the price +17% and its first sell moved it -35%. Agent activity lasts a **median of 39 s**. Finished Mayhem curves sit at a median 28% of a fresh coin's price. **Outside traders lost about 61.6k SOL in week one**, while only devs with 100 or more Mayhem coins roughly broke even. Since April 2026 the agent's buy/sell balance has been set "on a discretionary basis" ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-mayhem-mode)). Sandwich bots take about 6% of outside SOL on Mayhem coins. Only 158 of 7,958 Mayhem graduations filled the normal 85 SOL pool. The agent lost about 39k SOL overall, but it made 14.7k SOL on PumpSwap, the one venue where it came out ahead.

The only idea with a mechanism behind it is narrow. A reset is visible in `UpdateMayhemVirtualParamsEvent` the moment it lands, so a bot already holding tokens could sell into an upward reset. Buying after a downward reset has no support, because curves tend to stay down once the agent stops. One open question needs a check: our Mayhem share (about 60% of SOL launches) is far above Bitquery's 20-30% monthly figure through early October. Either adoption rose, or serial-deployer spam is concentrated in our window. In practice, exclude Mayhem coins, or treat their first 1-3 minutes as uninformative, and price them only from event-reported reserves.

### Creator fees reward serial deployment; cashback shaves 0.6 points

Curve trades on SOL and USDC coins cost **1.25% per side (0.95% protocol + 0.30% creator)**. The agent pays nothing ([pump-public-docs fees](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/fees.png)). The creator's 0.30% on all outside volume is part of why serial deployers can break even while their buyers lose, so a high deployer launch count is a negative signal. Cashback coins route the creator fee to the trader, which cuts a round trip from about 2.5% to about **1.9%**, provided our transactions include the `UserVolumeAccumulator` accounts. **New cashback coins can no longer be created**; existing ones keep paying, and holder-rewards coins replace them ([pump-public-docs cashback](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/PUMP_CASHBACK_README.md)). Decode `is_cashback_enabled` and re-run our cost model on that subset. It will not create an edge, but it moves breakeven on the coins it applies to. Non-SOL quotes deserve their own split before any SOL-based filter is applied, using `quote_mint` and `depth` from the CreateEvent. A multi-hop round trip through a pump-coin quote costs at least about 5% at depth 1, which rules out stale-parent arbitrage at our latency ([pump-public-docs](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/instructions/CREATE_WITH_PUMP_COIN_QUOTE.md)).

## Ranked tests for our recordings

The ranking weighs three things: the chance of a real edge at our latency and size, the strength of the evidence, and how much new data each test needs. Every test should hold out a later time block, never a random split, because of the temporal decay shown in [arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02823). Each should also be checked against the (vSol/115)² breakeven where relevant.

| Rank | Test | Why it ranks here | Extra data needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Walk-forward slow-wallet copy** (ARu4n5mF profile: entry ≥60 s, hold ≥5 min, ≥30 coins, non-deployer, non-sniper), with a decay curve over 1-10 slots, 2 s, 5 s and 30 s | The one copy case the literature has not tested; the mechanism says delay costs slow leaders little; buildable entirely from our fields | 7-14 more days of collection for period A plus 7 for period B; optional SOL transfers for finalists |
| 2 | **Entry-slot PnL decomposition and counterparty map** (who sells into a 30 s entry; does our data match the 65% dev-sell-within-1-min figure?) | Cheap diagnostic that sets the upper bound for every later-entry idea and calibrates the avoid filters | None |
| 3 | **Ride the BOOST bid at 0.5 SOL** (hold through the last 5 SOL of the curve and sell into the 5-minute TWAP) plus the post-BOOST dip at t > 5 min | Only mechanism with measured profit (completers); runs on a minutes scale a 1-2 s bot can reach | PumpSwap swap collector on deterministic pool addresses for ≥60 min per graduate; tagging of BOOST agent buys; confirmation that BOOST is still live |
| 4 | **Avoid-filter stack on the base entry** (dev-owned share at entry, factory buy sizes, wash share, dev-sold exit, Mayhem exclusion, KOL-linked buyers) | Best-supported loss reducer; becomes the feature layer for tests 1 and 3 | None, except free KOL wallet lists for the KOL part |
| 5 | **Last-SOL curve dynamics and stalled-curve census** (flow and price in the 70-85 real-SOL bins; who completes curves; how often others complete first) | Tests the depth-discontinuity dump and sizes the completer opportunity; the capital need keeps the full completer play out of reach | None for the entry side; PumpSwap data for exits |

Drop these: launch sniping, market making on the curve, LP on fresh PumpSwap pools, KOL front-running, multi-hop quote arbitrage, and any further mining of entry rules on curve features alone.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the problem. The question is not which entry rule works, since the market reliably punishes entries that 30 s of curve data can describe. The question is whose information still holds after a 1-2 s delay and a 2.5% fee. Most profit in pump.fun goes to insiders or to the fastest bots, and our failed tests mark how far the latecomer's share reaches, not a method error. Two places can still hide an edge. One is the behavior of slow, unlisted wallets, which nobody has studied at our delays. The other is protocol-scheduled order flow such as BOOST, which is predictable by construction until pump.fun changes it.

Both depend on data we do not yet have, not on cleverer analysis. Test 1 needs weeks of history, so the Pi collector matters more than any rule we could mine now. Tests 3 and 5 need a small PumpSwap collector, the one infrastructure addition worth building. If both come back negative under a walk-forward design, that is strong evidence that a retail bot on pump.fun has no edge left to find.
